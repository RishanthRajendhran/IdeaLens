# DIFFERENT KINDS OF FORMAT

Some formats are primarily expository or persuasive. Others may be
narrative, instructional, interactive, conversational, promotional, legal,
list-based, data-oriented, or incomplete.

Interpret "function" in a way appropriate to the format:

- In discursive documents, a role may identify what a move contributes to
  an explanation, argument, account, or recommendation.
- In narrative documents, a role may identify what a move contributes to
  events, characters, setting, tension, perspective, or resolution.
- In interactive or multi-participant documents, a role may identify what
  a contribution does within the exchange.
- In instructional documents, a role may identify what a move contributes
  to a goal, procedure, condition, warning, or outcome.
- In listings or structured information, a role may identify an item's
  information-bearing purpose, such as identifying, defining, describing,
  grouping, comparing, or supplying an attribute.
- In incomplete or access-limited content, a role may identify the
  function of the substantive material or access-related notice that is
  actually present.

In any format, a bounded structural component may also have a role when its
inclusion performs a meaningful document-level function.

Such functions may include:

- summarizing content;
- supporting navigation;
- documenting sources;
- defining terminology;
- supplying supplementary material;
- disclosing relevant information;
- recording attribution or contribution;
- organizing material for reference or later use.

Identify the function performed by the component, not merely its
conventional name, visual form, markup, or location.

For example, the relevant function is documenting sources rather than
merely being formatted as a source list, or supplying supplementary
material rather than merely being labelled as an appendix.

Do not force every format into the structure of a conventional essay or
argument.

For multi-author, interactive, compiled, or automatically structured
formats, evaluate an item's function within the resulting document or
content instance. Do not assume that the document has a single writer or a
single unified intention.


# UNIT BEING LABELLED

The units to be labelled are composition-level and structural outline
items.

An outline item represents one locally continuous commitment in the
document's latent composition and structural plan.

A commitment may be:

1. SUBSTANTIVE: a claim, fact, event, example, method, finding, decision,
   recommendation, or other content contribution;

2. RHETORICAL: a move that frames, motivates, qualifies, supports,
   contrasts, interprets, or concludes other content;

3. STRUCTURAL: a bounded document component included to organize,
   navigate, attribute, document, supplement, disclose, or otherwise
   support the document's communicative function.

An outline item is not necessarily one:

- sentence;
- paragraph;
- section;
- heading;
- speaker turn;
- list entry;
- data field;
- citation or link;
- formatting block;
- markup command.

Examples of the intended scale include:

- advancing one claim together with its immediate qualification;
- presenting one distinct example or body of support;
- describing one action or procedure with its attached conditions;
- reporting one substantively unified event, observation, or outcome;
- identifying one constraint and its direct consequence;
- characterizing or synthesizing related material around one point;
- developing one coherent narrative or figurative move;
- representing one bounded component whose internal entries jointly
  perform the same documentary or structural function.

These examples illustrate unit scale only. They are not a required or
exhaustive role inventory.

Every proposed role must be suitable as the primary label for a complete
outline item at this level of granularity.

Do not propose roles whose natural unit would instead be:

- an individual word, marker, or phrase;
- an entire document regardless of its internal commitments;
- a purely visual or typographic element;
- a formatting command or markup token;
- a structural container considered only as a container, without regard to
  its communicative or documentary function.

A bounded component may receive a structural role when the component's
inclusion and function are themselves part of the document plan.


# FUNCTIONAL LENS

A role names the primary substantive, rhetorical, communicative,
information-bearing, or structural job that an outline item performs at its
location in the document.

Determine function contextually by considering:

- what the item contributes;
- why it is present at that point;
- how it relates to nearby moves or components;
- how it advances the document's overall communicative, informational, or
  documentary purpose.

For a bounded structural item, consider what would be lost if the component
were omitted, not merely how the component is formatted or conventionally
named.

A role may be defined by the item's relationship to another move, another
component, or the document's overall purpose, but the inventory does not
need to follow any particular discourse-analysis theory.

A role is not:

- the item's topic or subject area;
- the kind of entity it mentions;
- its grammatical form;
- its visual presentation;
- its markup syntax;
- its position alone;
- the conventional name of a section or structural container without a
  functional definition;
- a summary of its specific content.

The same kind of information may receive different roles in different
contexts because it can be used for different purposes.

For example, the same factual material could orient the reader, support a
claim, illustrate a concept, establish a problem, provide a comparison, or
motivate an action. The appropriate role depends on its primary function
in context.

A role may correspond to a recurring kind of bounded component when it
names the component's function rather than its appearance.

For example, a role may capture source documentation, navigation,
supplementation, attribution, disclosure, or summarization when that
function recurs in the format. It should not merely label an item as a
source list, appendix, menu, footnote, table, or other surface form.


# SINGLE PRIMARY ROLE

The later labelling stage will assign exactly one primary role to each
outline item.

The role represents the item's main composition-level or structural
function at that point in the document.

An item may incidentally produce secondary effects. Do not create
multi-label or compound roles to represent every possible effect.

If two functions could be independently planned and would both be central,
they should normally correspond to separate outline items rather than a
compound role.

If an item has one central function and secondary effects, its role should
name only the central function.

Do not propose compound role names such as:

- "Background/Evidence";
- "Claim and Recommendation";
- "Instruction/Warning";
- "Event plus Interpretation";
- "Description and Evaluation";
- "Summary/Navigation";
- "Attribution and Evidence."

Instead, define distinct roles with assignment tests that allow a later
classifier to select the primary one.

Roles need not be logically incapable of overlap, but they must support a
principled choice of one primary label in context.

Structural items also receive exactly one primary role.

When a bounded component contains substantive material, choose the function
that best explains the outline item as extracted. If the item primarily
states a claim, reports evidence, gives instructions, narrates an event, or
performs another substantive move, use the corresponding substantive role
even if the material appears inside a table, note, appendix, or other
component.

Use a structural role when the item's primary commitment is the inclusion
and document-level function of the component itself.

Do not assign a structural role merely because an item has a particular
visual form or appears inside a conventionally named section.


# GENERALITY

Every role must be a genuinely cross-topic function.

A role should retain the same meaning when applied to {{FORMAT}} documents
on substantially different subjects.

If a role only makes sense for one topic, domain, named entity, specialized
procedure, or narrow scenario, generalize it to the underlying function or
remove it.

Do not create separate roles tied only to:

- a particular product, organization, person, or place;
- one specialized procedure or technique;
- one type of object, source, record, or dataset;
- one legal, medical, technical, historical, commercial, or cultural
  scenario;
- one topic-specific kind of event or outcome.

A more general role may still cover those cases when they perform the same
function.

Before retaining a candidate role, internally imagine it applying to
outline items from at least three substantially different subject areas
plausible for {{FORMAT}}. If its functional meaning changes across those
applications, revise or remove it.

Cross-topic generality does not mean that every role must apply across
different formats.

A structural role may be characteristic of {{FORMAT}} as long as it can
retain the same function across different topics within that format.

Do not reject a structural role merely because the corresponding component
is conventional in only some formats or subtypes. Reject it when the
proposed role names a surface convention rather than a recurring function.


# FORMAT RELEVANCE

Roles should be characteristic or recurrent enough in {{FORMAT}} to be
useful for later classification.

Do not include a role merely because it could theoretically occur in a
document of this format.

A role may be:

- broadly common across the format;
- characteristic of a recurring subtype named or implied by the format
  description;
- uncommon overall but sufficiently distinctive and recurrent to matter
  when it appears.

A role does not need to occur in every subtype. However, a role that makes
sense only for a rare and narrowly defined scenario is too specific.

Do not exclude a genuine recurring function merely because it is absent
from the most familiar subtype of the format.

For structural roles, consider whether the corresponding document-level
function recurs often enough in the format to warrant a stable base role.
Do not create a structural role for every component that could possibly
appear.


# ROLE GRANULARITY

Keep roles at a comparable level of functional abstraction.

A role is too broad if it collapses primary functions that a later
classifier should distinguish.

Roles such as the following are normally too broad unless the format makes
a narrower interpretation clear:

- "Information";
- "Content";
- "Main Material";
- "Supporting Material";
- "Document Component";
- "Structure";
- "Other."

A role is too narrow if it describes:

- one specialized procedure;
- one particular type of source, record, or data;
- one topic-specific assertion or action;
- one narrow kind of event or outcome;
- one purely visual, typographic, markup-specific, or platform-specific
  realization with no distinct document-level function;
- one named entity or object class.

Prefer a parsimonious inventory that preserves the recurring functional
distinctions needed to label outline items.

Parsimony is secondary to functional distinctness. Do not create an
umbrella role that combines several independently assignable functions
merely to reduce the number of roles.

Do not create separate roles merely because the same function:

- appears in different sections or positions;
- concerns different topics or entities;
- uses different kinds of source material;
- is expressed with different terminology;
- appears in different document subtypes;
- is realized through different surface forms;
- appears in different kinds of containers.

Do not create separate structural roles for different surface components
when they perform the same underlying function.

Conversely, do not merge a recurring structural function into a broad role
such as "Other" or "Document Material" merely because it does not advance a
substantive claim.


# STRUCTURAL ROLE PRINCIPLES

A structural role is appropriate when a bounded component's inclusion and
document-level purpose are the primary commitment represented by the
outline item.

A structural role should name what the component accomplishes, such as:

- orienting or summarizing;
- enabling navigation;
- documenting external sources;
- supplying supplementary material;
- defining reference terminology;
- disclosing relevant information;
- recording attribution or contribution;
- collecting material for reference.

These are examples of possible functions, not a mandatory role list.

Do not create a structural role based only on:

- a component's conventional title;
- the presence of a heading;
- visual separation from surrounding content;
- a particular markup command;
- numbering or citation syntax;
- the use of a table, figure, note, list, or appendix form.

A table may primarily report a finding, compare alternatives, present
evidence, define values, or serve another substantive role. An appendix may
primarily supply a method, evidence, examples, or supplementary
documentation. Classify the item by its primary function, not its
container.

A bounded collection may receive one structural role when its entries
jointly perform the same document-level function. Do not create one role
per internal entry unless those entries are themselves extracted as
independent outline items with different primary functions.


# ROLE DISTINCTNESS

Every pair of roles must support a principled single-label choice.

Roles do not need to be logically incapable of overlap, but their
definitions, assignment tests, and contrasts must explain which role should
be primary when multiple functions appear plausible.

Retain a role only if:

1. it represents a recurring function in {{FORMAT}};
2. it plausibly applies across multiple subject areas;
3. it labels a complete composition-level or structural outline item;
4. it is meaningfully distinguishable from every other retained role;
5. merging it with its nearest alternative would erase a distinction that
   matters during later labelling;
6. if it is structural, it names a recurring document-level function rather
   than a container, marker, or formatting convention.

Merge or remove:

- synonyms;
- near-synonyms without a stable assignment difference;
- narrower roles fully covered by another role;
- roles distinguished only by document position;
- roles distinguished only by topic or entity;
- roles distinguished only by surface realization;
- roles distinguished only by the visual or markup form of a component;
- structural labels that do not specify what function the component
  performs;
- compound roles combining independently assignable functions.

Do not merge roles merely because they often occur together or in
succession. Functions that commonly co-occur may still require separate
roles if they represent different primary jobs.

Do not merge a structural role with a substantive role merely because the
structural component contains substantive material. Distinguish them by
the primary function of the extracted item.


# NAMING

Give each role a short name of one to three words.

The name must identify the function itself rather than:

- describe a narrow scenario;
- summarize particular content;
- name a topic, domain, or entity;
- name a document location;
- name a surface form without identifying its function;
- name a markup or formatting convention;
- combine multiple roles with a slash, ampersand, "and," or "plus."

Use concise functional names in title case.

Names should be interpretable across different topics within the format.

For structural roles, name the document-level function rather than the
conventional component.

Prefer function-oriented names expressing purposes such as navigation,
documentation, supplementation, attribution, disclosure, definition, or
summarization over names that merely identify a visual container or markup
form.

Do not mechanically use these example terms as roles unless the functions
are genuinely recurrent and distinct in {{FORMAT}}.


# DEFINITION

For each role, write one sentence defining the content or component it
labels.

The definition must:

- identify the role's primary function;
- be general across topics;
- apply at the outline-item level;
- avoid relying on a particular section heading or document position;
- avoid relying on a particular entity or scenario;
- avoid defining the role only through examples;
- avoid treating visual or markup form as sufficient for assignment.

For a structural role, the definition must state:

- what document-level function the component performs;
- what kind of bounded commitment the role labels;
- why the function is more than formatting, position, or container type.


# ASSIGNMENT TEST

For each role, write one sentence stating when it should be selected as an
item's single primary role.

Frame the assignment test around the item's main contribution at that point
in the document.

Use this general test:

"If this item were removed, what primary substantive, rhetorical, or
structural function would disappear from the document at this point?"

The assignment test should help a later classifier choose among plausible
roles rather than merely repeat the definition.

It should identify what makes the role primary, not every secondary effect
the item may produce.

For a structural role, the assignment test should explain what
document-level function would be lost if the bounded component were
omitted.

It should also prevent the role from being selected merely because an item
appears in a particular kind of container.


# DISTINGUISHED FROM

For each role, identify its nearest likely competitor or competitors and
state the decisive functional difference in one sentence.

Focus on distinctions that could plausibly cause disagreement during
single-label assignment.

Do not distinguish roles merely by:

- wording;
- document position;
- topic or subject area;
- named entities;
- document length;
- formatting;
- markup syntax;
- the presence of a quotation, citation, link, speaker tag, field name, or
  other surface marker.

For structural roles, distinguish the role from:

- substantive roles that may appear inside the component;
- other structural roles with different document-level purposes;
- surface forms that do not independently determine function.

Do not treat the presence of a source marker, link, table, heading, note,
list, appendix, or markup command as sufficient evidence for a structural
role.


# INVENTORY SELF-CHECK

Before returning the inventory, review it as a complete role system.


## Coverage

Would the inventory cover the major recurring substantive, rhetorical, and
bounded structural functions found across {{FORMAT}} documents and the
recurring subtypes indicated by the format description?

In particular, consider whether the format commonly contains bounded
components whose organizational, navigational, attributional,
documentary, supplementary, disclosure, or reference function would not be
covered by the substantive roles.

Do not seek exhaustive coverage of every rare convention.

Do not add structural roles merely to cover every possible formatting
element or component.


## Format breadth

Has the inventory been based too heavily on one familiar subtype of
{{FORMAT}}?

If so, revise it to cover other recurring subtypes without introducing
roles that are merely subtype labels.


## Cross-topic generality

Could every role retain the same functional meaning across several
substantially different subjects?

Remove or generalize roles that fail this test.

For structural roles, test whether the same document-level function remains
stable across different topics within the format.


## Single-label usability

Could a later classifier usually choose one primary role for an item using
the definitions, assignment tests, and contrasts?

Clarify, split, or merge roles whose boundaries remain arbitrary.

Do not solve ambiguity by creating compound roles.

For an item inside a bounded component, could the classifier distinguish
between the component's structural function and the substantive function
of its contents?


## Non-redundancy

Are any two roles synonyms, near-synonyms, or distinctions without a stable
classification consequence?

If so, merge or remove them.

Are any structural roles merely alternate names for different containers
that perform the same function?

If so, merge them at the level of their shared function.


## Functional purity

Does every role name a substantive, rhetorical, communicative,
information-bearing, or structural function rather than a topic, entity,
position, formatting unit, markup command, or surface form?

A structural role is functionally pure when it identifies what a bounded
component accomplishes for the document rather than merely what the
component is called or how it is displayed.

Revise any role that fails this test.


## Structural coverage

Could every recurring bounded structural item likely to be produced by the
outline extractor receive a meaningful primary role?

Consider whether the format commonly contains components that:

- summarize;
- support navigation;
- document sources;
- define terminology;
- provide supplementary material;
- disclose relevant information;
- record attribution or contribution;
- organize material for reference.

Do not require a separate role for each component type. Group components
when they perform the same underlying function, and separate them when
their primary document-level functions differ.


## Comparable abstraction

Are the roles at approximately the same functional level?

Revise any inventory that mixes broad umbrella categories with very narrow
scenario-specific or component-specific roles.

Do not reject structural roles merely because they concern the document's
architecture. Instead, ensure that they name functions at a level
comparable to the substantive and rhetorical roles.


## Minimal sufficiency

Could any role be removed without losing a recurring and practically useful
functional distinction?

If yes, remove or merge it.

Would merging any remaining pair erase a distinction that would matter
during later single-label assignment?

If yes, keep them separate.

Could any role be divided only by introducing topic-specific,
position-specific, subtype-specific, container-specific, or overly narrow
distinctions?

If yes, keep it unified.

Parsimony does not justify merging a recurring structural function into an
unrelated substantive role.
