# TASK

Assign one primary functional role to every outline item in one
{{FORMAT}} document.

The outline may contain:

- substantive items;
- rhetorical items;
- bounded structural items.

The base role vocabulary below was proposed from general knowledge of the
format before any example documents were examined. Prefer a base role
whenever it correctly captures an item's primary function.

Invent a new candidate role only when the base vocabulary contains a
genuine functional gap and every base role would misstate what the item is
primarily doing.


# FORMAT

Format: {{FORMAT}}

# FUNCTIONAL PRINCIPLE

A role names the primary substantive, rhetorical, communicative,
information-bearing, or structural job that an outline item performs at its
location in the document.

Determine the role contextually by considering:

- what the item contributes;
- why it appears at that point;
- how it relates to preceding and following items;
- how it advances the document's overall communicative, informational, or
  documentary purpose;
- for a bounded component, what document-level function would disappear if
  the component were omitted.

The same kind of information may receive different roles in different
contexts.

For example, factual material could orient the reader, support a claim,
illustrate an idea, report an outcome, provide a comparison, or document a
source. Its role depends on how it is used.

Likewise, the same kind of document component may perform different
functions. A table may report a finding, present evidence, compare
alternatives, define values, or organize reference information. Its visual
or structural form does not determine its role.


# KINDS OF OUTLINE ITEM

An outline item may represent:

1. SUBSTANTIVE CONTENT

   A claim, fact, event, example, method, finding, decision,
   recommendation, instruction, or other content contribution.

2. RHETORICAL FUNCTION

   A move that frames, motivates, qualifies, supports, contrasts,
   interprets, or concludes other content.

3. STRUCTURAL FUNCTION

   A bounded component included to organize, navigate, attribute, document,
   supplement, disclose, summarize, or otherwise support the document's
   communicative purpose.

These categories guide interpretation only. Do not output them as
additional labels.

Classify every item according to its single primary function, regardless of
which broad category that function belongs to.


# SINGLE PRIMARY ROLE

Assign exactly one role to each item.

Choose the role representing the item's primary composition-level or
structural function: the main job that would disappear at that point if the
item were removed.

An item may have secondary rhetorical, substantive, or structural effects.
Do not assign or invent additional roles for those effects.

If several roles appear plausible, choose the one that best explains why
the item is present at that particular point, rather than merely describing:

- what kind of information it contains;
- what topic it concerns;
- what section it appears in;
- what visual or markup form it uses.

Do not create compound roles such as:

- "Background/Evidence";
- "Claim and Recommendation";
- "Instruction/Warning";
- "Finding plus Interpretation";
- "Summary/Navigation";
- "Attribution and Evidence";
- "Supplement and Method."


# STRUCTURAL ITEMS

A structural role is appropriate when the primary commitment represented by
the item is the inclusion and document-level function of a bounded
component.

Possible structural functions include:

- summarizing content;
- enabling navigation;
- documenting sources;
- defining terminology for reference;
- supplying supplementary material;
- disclosing relevant information;
- recording attribution or contribution;
- organizing material for later reference.

These are examples of possible functions, not a mandatory role list.

Do not assign a structural role merely because an item:

- appears inside a table, figure, note, appendix, source section, list, or
  other component;
- contains a citation, link, cross-reference, or source name;
- corresponds to a heading or section;
- has a particular visual or markup form;
- occurs at the beginning or end of the document.

When a bounded component contains substantive material, choose the role
that best explains the extracted item's primary commitment.

For example:

- If an item primarily reports a result, use the relevant result or finding
  role even if the result appeared in a table.
- If an item primarily presents support for a claim, use the relevant
  evidence role even if the material appeared in a figure or note.
- If an item primarily describes a procedure, use the relevant procedural
  or method role even if the material appeared in an appendix.
- If an item primarily records that a bounded component documents sources,
  supplies supplementary material, enables navigation, or performs another
  document-level function, use the corresponding structural role.

Classify by function rather than container.

# ASSIGNMENT PROCEDURE

First inspect the complete ordered item list and the global themes.

Do not classify each item in isolation.

Then:

1. Determine the primary function of each item in the context of the
   complete document.

2. Determine whether the item's primary commitment is substantive,
   rhetorical, or structural, without outputting that broad category.

3. Compare the function with the definitions, assignment tests, and
   distinctions in the base vocabulary.

4. Use the exact name of the best-fitting base role when one correctly
   captures the function.

5. Identify any genuine substantive, rhetorical, or structural functions
   that the base vocabulary does not cover.

6. Consolidate equivalent missing functions into one candidate role before
   assigning invented roles to items.

7. Recheck all items after identifying invented roles so that an invented
   role discovered later is also used for earlier items with the same
   primary function.

8. Return assignments in the original item order.


# USING BASE ROLES

Use a base role when its definition and assignment test correctly capture
the item's primary function.

Do not invent a role merely because:

- a base role is broader than the item's specific content;
- the item concerns an unusual topic, entity, or scenario;
- the item has secondary effects not named by the role;
- the item occurs in an unusual document position;
- two base roles appear plausible;
- a narrower label could be created for this particular item;
- the item appears in a distinctive visual or structural container;
- the item contains a citation, source name, link, table, note, heading, or
  other surface marker;
- a structural component has a conventional name not used by the base
  vocabulary.

When two base roles appear plausible, use their definitions,
`assignment_test` fields, and `distinguished_from` fields to choose the
better primary role.

For a base role:

- reproduce its name exactly;
- set `is_invented` to `false`.


# DISTINGUISHING SUBSTANTIVE AND STRUCTURAL ROLES

When both a substantive role and a structural role appear plausible, ask:

"What does this outline item primarily commit the reconstructed document to
include?"

Use a substantive or rhetorical role when the item primarily commits the
document to:

- make a claim;
- report a fact, event, observation, or result;
- present evidence;
- give an example;
- describe a method or procedure;
- offer an interpretation;
- issue an instruction or recommendation;
- perform another content-level move.

Use a structural role when the item primarily commits the document to
include a bounded component for a document-level purpose, such as:

- collecting sources;
- supporting navigation;
- providing supplementary material;
- recording disclosures or attributions;
- organizing reference information;
- summarizing another portion of the document as a component.

Do not classify by the component's name alone.

A component may have a structural function even when it contains
substantive material, but that structural function should be selected only
when it is the primary commitment represented by the outline item.


# INVENTING A ROLE

Invent a role only when every base role would mischaracterize the item's
primary function.

An invented role may represent a missing substantive, rhetorical, or
structural function.

Every invented role must:

- name a recurring communicative, information-bearing, rhetorical, or
  document-level structural function;
- be general across topics;
- apply to complete composition-level or structural outline items;
- be distinct from every base role and every other invented role;
- avoid referring to the item's specific subject matter;
- use a short one-to-three-word name;
- not combine multiple independently assignable functions.

An invented structural role must:

- name what a bounded component accomplishes for the document;
- remain meaningful across different topics within {{FORMAT}};
- be more than the conventional name of a section, container, or markup
  form;
- not be created solely because an item uses a particular visual format,
  citation style, or structural marker.

Do not invent a role merely to name:

- a bibliography, appendix, table, figure, footnote, heading, list, or
  other container as such;
- an individual citation, link, reference entry, label, or marker;
- a formatting or markup convention;
- a component's position in the document.

Instead, identify the underlying function that the component performs.

Do not target any particular invention rate. Prefer a base role whenever
it fits, but do not force-fit an item when the inventory has a genuine
functional gap.

Before finalizing invented roles:

- merge synonymous or near-duplicate inventions;
- use the same invented role for every item with the same primary function;
- verify that no base role already covers the function;
- verify that the role is not merely a narrower topic-specific or
  container-specific subtype of a base role;
- define how the invention differs from its nearest base or invented role.

For every invented role, supply:

- `name`;
- `definition`;
- `assignment_test`;
- `distinguished_from`.


# INVENTED ROLE DEFINITION

For an invented substantive or rhetorical role, `definition` must state
what content-level function the role labels.

For an invented structural role, `definition` must state:

- what bounded commitment the role labels;
- what document-level function the component performs;
- why the function is not merely formatting, position, or container type.

The definition must be one sentence and remain general across topics.


# INVENTED ROLE ASSIGNMENT TEST

The `assignment_test` must state when the invented role should be selected
as an item's single primary role.

Use this general test:

"If this item were removed, what primary substantive, rhetorical, or
structural function would disappear at this point?"

For an invented structural role, explain what document-level function would
be lost if the bounded component were omitted.

The assignment test must prevent selection based only on visual form,
markup, component name, or document position.


# INVENTED ROLE DISTINCTION

The `distinguished_from` field must identify the nearest base or invented
competitor and state the decisive functional difference.

For a structural role, distinguish it from:

- substantive roles that may appear inside the component;
- other structural roles serving different document-level purposes;
- surface forms or containers that do not independently determine
  function.

Do not distinguish roles merely by wording, topic, position, formatting,
markup syntax, citation presence, or container name.


# CONSISTENCY ACROSS ITEMS

Use the same role for items that perform the same primary function, even
when they:

- concern different topics or entities;
- appear in different sections;
- use different kinds of evidence or examples;
- are realized through different visual forms;
- occur inside different kinds of bounded components.

Do not use the same role merely because items:

- share a topic;
- use the same kind of container;
- contain similar keywords;
- occur in similar positions;
- include citations or links.

Functional equivalence, not surface similarity, determines role reuse.


# SELF-CHECK

Before returning the assignments, verify:

1. Every outline item has exactly one role.

2. Every non-invented role name exactly matches a base role.

3. Every invented role represents a genuine functional gap rather than a
   narrower topic-specific, position-specific, or container-specific
   subtype of a base role.

4. No invented roles are synonymous or near-duplicates.

5. Items performing the same primary function use the same role.

6. No compound or multi-label roles appear.

7. Assignments reflect the complete document context rather than isolated
   keywords or item content.

8. Structural roles are assigned by document-level function rather than
   visual form, markup, section name, or container type.

9. An item inside a structural component receives a substantive role when
   its primary commitment is substantive.

10. A bounded component receives a structural role when its inclusion and
    document-level function are the primary commitment represented by the
    item.

11. The presence of citations, links, tables, figures, notes, headings,
    appendices, or source lists has not by itself determined any role.

12. Every invented structural role states what function the component
    performs rather than merely naming the component.

13. Item IDs and order match the supplied outline exactly.
