# TASK

Below are candidate role labels used while assigning one primary functional
role to outline items across {{N_DOCS}} documents in the "{{FORMAT}}"
format.

The candidates come from two sources:

1. BASE roles proposed from general knowledge of the format before any real
   documents were examined;

2. INVENTED roles proposed within individual documents when no base role
   correctly captured an item's primary function.

Your task is to consolidate these candidates into the FINAL role vocabulary
for {{FORMAT}}.

The final inventory must cover recurring substantive, rhetorical, and
bounded structural functions while remaining parsimonious,
cross-topic, single-label usable, and grounded in the supplied definitions,
assignment tests, usage counts, and real outline-item examples.


# FORMAT

Format: {{FORMAT}}

Documents examined: {{N_DOCS}}


# INPUT CANDIDATES

Each candidate is identified by a unique `candidate_id` and includes:

- its origin: base or invented;
- its original role name;
- its usage count;
- its definition;
- its assignment test;
- its nearest functional distinction;
- up to several real examples of items assigned that role.

Candidate IDs, rather than role names, are the authoritative identifiers.

Different candidates may have the same name. A shared name is evidence of
possible equivalence, but it does not prove equivalence. Compare their
definitions, assignment tests, distinctions, and examples before merging
them.

Likewise, candidates with different names may describe the same underlying
function and should be merged when the evidence supports doing so.


# FUNCTIONAL PRINCIPLE

A role names the primary substantive, rhetorical, communicative,
information-bearing, or structural function that an outline item performs
at its location in a document.

The final roles must classify composition-level outline items, not:

- topics;
- named entities;
- grammatical forms;
- document positions;
- visual containers;
- formatting or markup conventions;
- specific scenarios;
- secondary rhetorical effects.

Every final role must support assignment as an item's single primary role.


# SINGLE-ROLE USABILITY

The final vocabulary will later be used for single-label classification.

Each final role must represent one coherent primary function.

Roles need not be logically incapable of overlap, but their:

- definitions;
- assignment tests;
- distinctions from nearby roles;

must support a principled choice of one primary role in context.

Do not create compound roles such as:

- "Background/Evidence";
- "Claim and Recommendation";
- "Instruction/Warning";
- "Finding plus Interpretation";
- "Summary/Navigation";
- "Attribution and Evidence."

Do not solve ambiguity by joining several distinguishable functions into
one compound label.


# EVIDENCE AVAILABLE

Use all of the following evidence:

1. DEFINITIONS

   What function each candidate was intended to represent.

2. ASSIGNMENT TESTS

   When each candidate was intended to be selected as an item's single
   primary role.

3. FUNCTIONAL DISTINCTIONS

   How each candidate was intended to differ from its nearest competitors.

4. REAL EXAMPLES

   How the candidate was actually used on composition-level outline items.

5. USAGE COUNTS

   How often the candidate was assigned across the examined documents.

No single source of evidence is decisive by itself.


# COUNT ASYMMETRY

Usage counts are meaningful but are not directly comparable across
candidate origins.

Base roles were available for reuse in every document and were explicitly
preferred whenever they fit.

Invented roles were created independently within individual documents only
after the base vocabulary was judged inadequate. One recurring missing
function may therefore be fragmented across several low-count invented
roles with different names.

Consequently:

- do not treat a high base-role count as proof that its definition
  correctly describes every assigned item;
- do not treat a low invented-role count as proof that its function is
  unimportant or overly narrow;
- look for semantically equivalent invented candidates across documents
  before judging recurrence;
- consider the combined usage of candidates proposed for merger;
- treat repeated independent invention of equivalent functions as evidence
  that the base vocabulary lacked a recurring role;
- use count as one signal, not as a substitute for semantic and functional
  fit.


# WHEN TO MERGE

Merge candidates when one coherent final role can correctly describe all
of their assigned examples and intended functions.

A merge is appropriate when:

- the candidates describe the same underlying primary function;
- their differences are only terminological;
- one is a topic-specific or scenario-specific version of the other;
- one is a narrow special case fully covered by a broader functional role;
- several independently invented candidates express the same missing
  function;
- different structural container names express the same underlying
  document-level function.

A valid merged role must have:

- one coherent definition;
- one usable assignment test;
- one stable distinction from neighboring roles;
- a natural relationship to every candidate it subsumes.

Every real example associated with a merged candidate must remain a natural
instance of the final role.


# WHEN NOT TO MERGE

Do not merge candidates merely because they:

- are closely related;
- frequently occur next to each other;
- contribute to the same broad goal;
- concern the same topic;
- use similar words;
- occur in similar document positions;
- appear in the same kind of section or container;
- share a broad family such as support, framing, instruction, narrative,
  evidence, or structure;
- would produce a smaller vocabulary if combined.

Keep roles separate when the distinction changes the primary reason an item
is present.

In particular, preserve a distinction when:

- the roles answer different assignment questions;
- removing the corresponding items would remove different functions;
- one role cannot naturally describe the other's real examples;
- a later classifier would need the distinction to choose the correct
  single primary role;
- combining them would require a vague umbrella definition;
- both functions are independently well attested and consistently used.

Relatedness is not assignment equivalence.


# BROADENING AND GENERALIZATION

A candidate may be renamed or generalized without being merged when its
original name is too topic-bound, scenario-bound, container-specific, or
narrow.

Generalize to the underlying recurring function.

A generalized final role must still correctly describe every real item
assigned to the original candidate. Do not generalize so far that the role
becomes equivalent to:

- "Information";
- "Content";
- "Supporting Material";
- "Document Component";
- "Other."

Do not preserve an invented label unchanged when it merely names:

- a particular subject;
- a named entity;
- one specialized technique;
- one narrow event or scenario;
- a visual container;
- a section title;
- a formatting or markup convention.

Name the recurring function instead.


# STRUCTURAL ROLES

Some candidates may label bounded structural items rather than ordinary
substantive or rhetorical moves.

A structural role is valid when it names a recurring document-level
function, such as:

- summarizing content;
- supporting navigation;
- documenting sources;
- defining reference terminology;
- supplying supplementary material;
- disclosing relevant information;
- recording attribution or contribution;
- organizing material for reference.

These are examples of possible functions, not a required role list.

For structural candidates:

- identify what the component accomplishes for the document;
- merge labels that merely name different containers performing the same
  function;
- do not retain roles based only on section names, visual forms, markup
  commands, or document positions;
- do not merge a structural role into a substantive role merely because
  the component contains substantive material;
- do not retain separate structural roles when their only difference is
  whether the component appeared as a table, note, appendix, list, or other
  container.

Distinguish structural and substantive roles by the primary commitment
represented by the real outline items.

If an item primarily reports a result, presents evidence, gives an
instruction, or advances a claim, its role is substantive even if it
appeared inside a structural component.

If an item primarily records that a bounded component documents,
supplements, navigates, attributes, discloses, or otherwise supports the
document, its role may be structural.


# BASE-ROLE TREATMENT

Base roles are top-down hypotheses, not automatically correct final roles.

A base role with substantial coherent usage is evidence for retaining it,
but usage alone does not prevent merger or revision when its function
duplicates another role.

A base role with low or zero usage may still represent a valid function
that was absent from the examined sample.

For every base candidate:

- retain it as a distinct final role when it represents a coherent,
  potentially useful function not covered elsewhere;
- merge it when another final role genuinely and fully covers its
  function;
- place it in `unretained_base_roles` only when it has zero observed usage
  and there is insufficient empirical basis to include it in the final
  vocabulary.

Do not place a used base candidate in `unretained_base_roles`.

Do not force a zero-use base candidate into a semantically mismatched final
role merely to preserve it.

A zero-use base candidate retained in the final vocabulary may have no real
example, but its definition and assignment test must still describe a
distinct and plausible function for {{FORMAT}}.


# INVENTED-ROLE TREATMENT

An invented candidate is evidence that the base vocabulary may have missed
a function, but it is not automatically a valid final role.

For each invented candidate:

- compare it with every semantically nearby base and invented candidate;
- merge it when an existing or broader final role genuinely covers it;
- generalize it when it is topic-bound or container-bound;
- retain it distinctly when its examples show a recurring function not
  captured elsewhere.

Repeated independent inventions with similar definitions and examples are
strong evidence for one missing recurring role, even when each individual
candidate has a low count.

Every invented candidate has observed usage and must be subsumed by exactly
one final role.


# FINAL ROLE NAMING

Give each final role a concise one-to-three-word name in Title Case.

The name must identify the function itself rather than:

- a topic;
- a named entity;
- a document position;
- a narrow scenario;
- a visual container;
- a markup convention;
- multiple functions joined with a slash, ampersand, "and," or "plus."

For structural roles, name the document-level function rather than the
component's conventional title or surface form.

Reuse a clear existing candidate name when appropriate. Rename candidates
when a more general, precise, or consistent functional name is needed.


# FINAL ROLE DEFINITION

For each final role, write one sentence defining the content or component
it labels.

The definition must:

- identify one coherent primary function;
- cover every candidate and real example subsumed by the role;
- remain general across topics;
- apply at the outline-item level;
- avoid relying on a particular document position, section name, entity,
  scenario, or surface form;
- remain specific enough to support classification.

For a structural role, define what document-level function the bounded
component performs rather than what the component is called or how it is
formatted.


# FINAL ASSIGNMENT TEST

For each final role, write one sentence stating when it should be selected
as an item's single primary role.

Use this general test:

"If this item were removed, what primary function would disappear from the
document at this point?"

The assignment test must:

- identify the role's primary function;
- help distinguish it from plausible alternatives;
- apply naturally to all real examples subsumed by the role;
- avoid merely repeating the definition.

For a structural role, state what document-level function would be lost if
the bounded component were omitted.


# FINAL DISTINCTION

For each final role, identify its nearest likely competitor or competitors
and state the decisive functional difference in one sentence.

Use distinctions that matter for single-label assignment.

Do not distinguish roles merely by:

- wording;
- topic;
- document position;
- named entities;
- formatting;
- markup syntax;
- container type;
- the presence of citations, links, headings, tables, figures, or notes.

If two proposed final roles cannot be distinguished by a stable assignment
test, merge or redefine them.


# COMPLETE CANDIDATE PARTITION

Every input candidate must be accounted for exactly once.

Each candidate with observed usage must appear in the `subsumes` array of
exactly one final role.

Each zero-use base candidate must appear exactly once in either:

- the `subsumes` array of one final role; or
- `unretained_base_roles`.

No candidate may:

- appear in more than one final role;
- appear both in a final role and in `unretained_base_roles`;
- be omitted from both locations.

Use the exact `candidate_id` supplied in the input. Do not use the role name
as the identifier.


# EXAMPLES

Every output example must be selected from the real examples supplied in
the input candidates.

Do not:

- invent examples;
- invent document or item IDs;
- paraphrase example content;
- combine fragments from different examples;
- attribute an example to a candidate that did not supply it.

Choose examples that:

- clearly illustrate the final role;
- represent the range of candidates subsumed by it;
- help distinguish the role from nearby alternatives.

A final role may have no examples only when all candidates it subsumes have
zero observed usage.

Use no more than three examples per final role.


# CONSOLIDATION PROCEDURE

Perform the consolidation in this order:

1. Inspect all candidates, definitions, assignment tests, distinctions,
   counts, and examples.

2. Identify candidates that are clearly equivalent despite naming
   differences.

3. Identify independently invented candidates that collectively indicate
   one recurring missing function.

4. Identify topic-specific, scenario-specific, and container-specific
   candidates that should be generalized.

5. Identify candidates that are related but must remain separate for
   single-label assignment.

6. Identify structural candidates and distinguish their document-level
   functions from the substantive content of their components.

7. Form provisional final-role groups.

8. For each group, verify that one definition and assignment test naturally
   cover every candidate and example in the group.

9. Compare every pair of provisional final roles for redundancy and stable
   assignability.

10. Decide whether each zero-use base candidate should be retained, merged
    on semantic grounds, or listed as unretained.

11. Verify the complete candidate partition.

12. Return the final vocabulary.


# FINAL SELF-CHECK

Before returning the result, verify:

1. Every final role represents one coherent primary function.

2. Every final role is usable for single-label assignment.

3. No final role is merely a topic, entity, position, container, formatting
   convention, or surface form.

4. No two final roles are synonyms or near-synonyms without a stable
   assignment difference.

5. No distinct, well-supported functions were merged merely to reduce the
   inventory size.

6. No low-count candidate was retained merely because it had a distinctive
   name.

7. No low-count invented candidate was dismissed without checking for
   equivalent inventions elsewhere.

8. Count asymmetry between base and invented candidates was taken into
   account.

9. Every merged role naturally describes all of its supplied examples.

10. Every structural role names a document-level function rather than a
    component type.

11. Every used candidate appears in exactly one final role.

12. Every zero-use base candidate appears in exactly one final role or in
    `unretained_base_roles`.

13. Every output example comes directly from the supplied candidate
    evidence.

14. All `candidate_id` values are reproduced exactly.
